Abstract (5-Sentence Summary)

Editorial and SEO teams managing tens of thousands of URLs face severe bandwidth constraints when identifying which decaying pages warrant immediate manual intervention. Using the official anonymized production sample from the FlyRank search intelligence warehouse comprising 30,000 content items across 32 enterprise clients, we examined 90-day search visibility metrics, on-page structure, and keyword intent while enforcing a programmatic leakage harness to purge 13 label-derived and 30-day formula-leaking columns. We formulated content refresh prioritization as a decision-support triage task, evaluating an L2-regularized Logistic Regression scorer and a depth-constrained Random Forest benchmark under a strict client-grouped holdout split (25 training clients, 7 unseen test clients with zero domain overlap). Evaluated under a locked comparison contract on the 6,163 held-out test pages, our primary model achieved a Precision@10 of 70.0% and Precision@50 of 70.0%, outperforming the Week 4 heuristic striking-distance baseline by +50.0 and +30.0 percentage points respectively (a 3.5× lift at K=10 against a 51.1% test base rate). Finally, we weight calibrated decay probabilities by 90-day impression demand to route 10,670 actionable URLs into a four-archetype Action Playbook governed by automated human-in-the-loop safety guardrails.

Section 01

Introduction & Operational Problem Statement

Enterprise organic search portfolios routinely span 10,000 to 100,000+ published URLs, yet editorial and SEO teams typically have the human bandwidth to audit and rewrite only 10 to 50 pages per sprint. Across our 30,000-page production sample, 16,262 pages (54.21%) are in active decline, including 3,363 high-demand pages (≥5,000 impressions).

The Decision Supported

Rather than relying on crude static rules (such as flagging every page with CTR ≤ 0.5% in positions 4 to 20, which produces an 80% false-alarm rate in the top 10), our framework scores each URL's decay probability, weights it by 90-day impression volume, and assigns a concrete operational archetype and reason code.

Asymmetric Cost of a Wrong Call

False Positive (False Alarm): Wastes 4 to 8 hours of editorial rewriting on a stable URL experiencing normal seasonal noise (mitigated via a 5-minute human SERP check).
False Negative (Missed Decay): Permits a core revenue-driving pillar page to bleed rankings and organic demand unnoticed until recovery requires months of link and content remediation.

Section 03

Methodology, Leakage Harness & Locked Comparison Contract

1. Client-Grouped Split

Enforced via GroupShuffleSplit (80/20, seed 42) on client_id: 23,837 training rows across 25 clients and 6,163 held-out test rows across 7 unseen clients. We also run 5-fold GroupKFold cross-validation on the training partition.

2. Deliberate Leakage Harness

To prove our audit catches circular features, we ran a quarantined test injecting impressions_last_30d, impressions_prev_30d, and trend_pct. Average Precision spiked to 1.0000, verifying the harness detects formula leaks before locking the clean 32-feature run.

3. Fair Comparison Contract

All models and the Week 4 striking-distance heuristic (impressions_90d × (21 - avg_position)) are evaluated on the exact same 6,163 unseen client rows with deterministic tie-breaking (score DESC, impressions_90d DESC, content_id ASC).

Empirical Results vs. Baseline on Held-Out Clients

Section 05

Limitations, NIST AI RMF Framing & The Generalization Gap

  • Observational Association, Not Causal Proof: Our predictions reflect observed historical patterns. We do not claim that refreshing a page causally guarantees traffic recovery; causal attribution requires prospective A/B split testing.
  • Zero Algorithm Reverse-Engineering: We model content performance decay for editorial workflow triage only and make no claims regarding Google's proprietary ranking weights.
  • Same-Snapshot Horizon: Because trend_direction summarizes trajectory within the current release window, this framework operates as a cross-client decay detector rather than a multi-quarter forecasting oracle.

Section 06

Ranked Recommendations: The Content Action Playbook

We weight each page's calibrated decay probability by its 90-day search visibility (Priority Score = impressions_90d × model_probability) and segment the 30,000-URL inventory into 19,330 Stable Performers (64.4%) and 10,670 Actionable Candidates (35.6%) across four operational archetypes:

Operational Archetype Quantitative Rule Portfolio Volume Prescribed Editorial Action & Reason Code
Decaying High-Demand Core impressions_90d ≥ 5,000 & prob ≥ 0.60 3,027 URLs (10.1%) comprehensive_content_refresh (Update depth, intent & freshness)
Striking-Distance Opportunity 4 ≤ pos ≤ 20, imp ≥ 3k, ctr ≤ 0.5% 2,700 URLs (9.0%) review_title_meta_and_intent (Optimize H1/Title for SERP CTR)
High-Visibility CTR Deficit 1 ≤ pos ≤ 3, imp ≥ 3k, ctr ≤ 0.8% 200 URLs (0.7%) serp_snippet_optimization (Audit structured schema & snippets)
Thin / Zombie Content impressions_90d < 100 & age > 180d 4,743 URLs (15.8%) evaluate_consolidation_or_pruning (301 redirect or prune check)
